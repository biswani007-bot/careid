# 1. DOCUMENT PURPOSE

This document defines the proposed functional, technical and operational requirements of the **CareID Patient Mobile Application**.

The Patient App is the patient-facing application of the CareID healthcare ecosystem.

It enables patients to manage their healthcare identity, interact with authorized healthcare providers, access medical information, manage appointments, review treatment offers, participate in documented financing workflows, track prescriptions and referrals, and complete the QR-based interactions required by the original HEALTH VAULT 360 documentation.

The application must use the shared CareID Backend and authoritative database.

It must not introduce independent or hardcoded clinical, financial or administrative business rules.

## 1.1 Document Authority

The governing requirement sources are:

1. Original HEALTH VAULT 360 PRD/FSD.
2. Approved CareID change requests CR-02 through CR-08.
3. Approved CareID architecture and security decisions.
4. This Patient App specification after stakeholder approval.

**Conflict rule:** Where this document conflicts with an explicit approved requirement, the original approved requirement takes precedence until a formal change is authorized.

**Source-verification limitation:** The original PRD/FSD and change-request workbook were not available for direct inspection when this version was prepared. This document therefore distinguishes confirmed project decisions, proposed mobile design and requirements needing verification.

No unverified proposal is automatically an approved development requirement.

# 2. PRODUCT VISION

## 2.1 Product Definition

CareID Patient App is a secure mobile healthcare application that allows patients to interact with their healthcare providers and manage supported healthcare activities through a single digital platform.

The app should provide a simple, accessible interface while maintaining accurate integration with the Doctor App, Doctor Web, Admin Web and Backend.

## 2.2 Core Principles

1. **Patient-centered experience:** Prioritize frequently used patient services.
2. **Secure digital identity:** Protect patient identity and healthcare information.
3. **Documented QR integration:** Implement the required QR workflow exactly as approved.
4. **Shared clinical information:** Retrieve records from the authoritative Backend.
5. **Dynamic configuration:** Use Admin-managed healthcare configuration.
6. **Transparent financial information:** Display actual treatment and financing information.
7. **Reliable appointments:** Use genuine doctor and facility availability.
8. **Privacy and consent:** Enforce applicable access and consent requirements.
9. **No false confirmations:** Never show success before the Backend confirms an action.
10. **Scope control:** Do not add unsupported healthcare services.

# 7. PA-03 — MANDATORY QR SCANNING AND QR INTERACTION

## 7.1 Requirement Classification

**Mandatory source-verification requirement.**

The product owner has confirmed that Patient App QR scanning is required by the original documentation.

The exact sequence, actors, permissions and resulting workflow must be verified against the original HEALTH VAULT 360 PRD/FSD.

QR scanning must not be removed from scope merely because the Doctor App is being developed as a mobile companion.

## 7.2 Required QR Workflow Analysis

The implementation specification must identify:

1. Who generates or displays the QR code.
2. Which application scans the QR code.
3. What the QR code represents.
4. At which healthcare workflow stage scanning occurs.
5. Which Backend endpoint processes the scan.
6. What patient, doctor, facility or appointment context is established.
7. Whether the QR interaction requires consent or additional confirmation.
8. What successful scanning permits.
9. What happens when scanning fails.
10. How the result is synchronized across applications.

## 7.3 QR Interface Requirements

The Patient App must implement the documented QR capability, which may involve a scanner, QR display or both, depending on the approved source.

Where scanning is required, the application must support:

- Camera permission handling.
- QR scanning interface.
- Scan result validation.
- Loading and confirmation states.
- Invalid QR handling.
- Expired QR handling.
- Unauthorized QR handling.
- Clear success or failure feedback.

Where displaying a QR is required, the app must follow the approved identity and security rules.

**Do not assume that the Patient App must both generate and scan QR codes unless the source requires both.**

## 7.4 QR Security Requirements

- QR data must not automatically grant unrestricted clinical access.
- The Backend must validate the relevant patient or appointment context.
- Sensitive medical information should not be unnecessarily embedded in QR content.
- Expired or revoked QR interactions must be rejected where applicable.
- QR replay or reuse must follow the documented security model.
- Relevant scan events must be auditable where required.
- QR scanning must not bypass consent or authorization.

## 7.5 Cross-Application Integration

The documented QR interaction must be mapped across:

**Patient App ↔ CareID Backend ↔ Doctor App / Doctor Web**

Admin Web may participate where the original workflow requires administrative oversight.

## 7.6 QR Acceptance Criteria

**QR-001:** The correct documented QR workflow is implemented.

**QR-002:** Valid authorized QR interactions succeed.

**QR-003:** Invalid or unauthorized QR interactions fail safely.

**QR-004:** Scanning does not expose unrelated patient records.

**QR-005:** The Backend persists or processes the correct resulting state.

**QR-006:** Relevant Doctor App or Doctor Web state is synchronized.

**QR-007:** QR behavior is tested on actual supported mobile devices.

**QR-008:** The implementation is traceable to the original PRD/FSD section.

**Approval gate:** No final QR implementation contract may be approved without source verification.

## 13.3 Lifecycle Requirements

The Patient App must distinguish actual Backend states, including where applicable:

- Draft or not yet delivered.
- Dispatched.

- Accepted.
- Declined.
- Expired.

## 13.5 Acceptance Criteria

- Patient response persists.
- Doctor-facing status updates correctly.
- Expired or unauthorized actions are rejected.
- No duplicate acceptance events are created.

# 24. SHARED SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE

## 24.1 Application Responsibilities

### Patient App

Patient-facing healthcare interactions.

### Doctor App

Mobile clinical review, communication and approved clinical actions.

### Doctor Web

Detailed clinical workstation and advanced consultation workflows.

### Admin Web

Administrative configuration, oversight and management.

### Backend

Authentication, authorization, business logic, scheduling, financial calculations and persistent data access.

### Database

Authoritative source of clinical, administrative and workflow records.

## 24.2 Cross-Platform Consistency

An action performed in one application must be reflected appropriately in other applications.

Examples:

- Patient books an appointment → Doctor schedule updates.
- Doctor reschedules an appointment → Patient App reflects the change.
- Doctor dispatches an offer → Patient App shows the offer.
- Patient accepts an offer → Doctor Web and Doctor App show the actual response.
- Doctor records a valid clearance → Patient App shows the authorized readiness status.
- Prescription is collected → Future pickup reminders stop.

All synchronization must use actual Backend state.

# 26. PATIENT CONSULTATION AND TREATMENT LIFECYCLE

The proposed Patient App must participate in the approved CareID consultation lifecycle.

## Stage 1 — Patient Identification

Establish the correct patient context using the approved identity workflow, including documented QR interaction where required.

## Stage 2 — Appointment

Search for an eligible doctor or facility and book a genuine available appointment.

## Stage 3 — Consultation

Attend the appointment through the documented process and access permitted consultation information.

## Stage 4 — Treatment Offer

Receive and review an authorized treatment offer.

## Stage 5 — Patient Decision

Accept or decline the offer through the approved workflow.

## Stage 6 — Financing

Review or complete the applicable patient-side financing actions where supported.

## Stage 7 — Consent

Complete any required patient authorization independently of offer acceptance.

## Stage 8 — Clinical Readiness

View patient-accessible readiness information and outstanding requirements.

Clinical approval remains the responsibility of authorized clinicians.

## Stage 9 — Procedure or Follow-Up Booking

Participate in the documented booking process using actual available resources.

## Stage 10 — Follow-Up

Track appointments, prescriptions, referrals and relevant notifications.

**Important:** This is a proposed lifecycle map. The exact order, mandatory stages and QR integration points must be verified against the original PRD/FSD.

# 31. DEFINITION OF DONE

A Patient App feature is complete only when:

- The requirement is approved and traceable.
- The Flutter interface is implemented.
- Backend integration works.
- Authentication and authorization are enforced.
- Required data is dynamically loaded.
- Actions persist correctly.
- Relevant Doctor App and Doctor Web states synchronize.
- Error, loading and empty states are handled.
- Sensitive data is protected.
- Relevant negative tests pass.
- No hardcoded operational configuration is introduced.
- Actual mobile behavior is verified.
- Evidence is documented.
- Unrelated systems remain unchanged.

A successful Flutter build alone does not prove functional completion.
